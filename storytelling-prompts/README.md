# Storytelling Prompts

A collection of AI prompt frameworks for visual storytelling and design. Each prompt is designed to be pasted directly into Claude (or any AI assistant) as-is; the AI will interview you first, then generate the output.

---

## Prompts

### 1. [Storytelling & Image Prompt Builder](./storytelling-and-image-prompt-builder.md)

A staged interview that helps you develop a single, polished visual story concept from scratch, then turns it into one or more ready-to-use image generation prompts.

**Best for:** Developing one concept in depth for an ad, poster, packaging, campaign, or social post.

**How it works:**

The AI walks you through four stages:

- **Stage 1 (The core story):** Define what you're communicating, who the protagonist is (the brand, the product, the viewer, or an object), and what conflict or threat they face.
- **Stage 2 (The element of surprise):** Choose a surprise flavor (humor, poignancy, or suspense) and identify the specific "aha/get-it moment" the viewer has to close themselves.
- **Stage 3 (The visual toolbox):** Make deliberate choices across symbolism, color & mood, composition, style/era, and typography (including which word is the "protagonist word").
- **Stage 4 (Output format):** Specify whether this is a single image or series, and what medium or platform it's for.

**What you get:**

- A "visual story" paragraph describing the single moment in time the design will portray
- Headline/tagline options
- One detailed image-generation prompt per concept (or per variation if a series)
- A sanity check flagging any symbol or color choices that could carry unintended meaning

### 2. [Story Concepts & Storyboard Prompt Builder](./story-concepts-and-storyboard-prompt-builder.md)

A brainstorming-first prompt that generates **three distinct visual story concepts**, each using a different creative strategy, so a client can compare directions before committing to one.

**Best for:** Early-stage concepting when you need options to present, not a single polished direction.

**How it works:**

The AI asks eight questions one at a time, covering:

- The project, product, or campaign
- Target audience resistance or indifference
- A realistic, measurable short-term goal (not a lofty outcome)
- Who the protagonist is, or whether to explore multiple protagonist types
- The opposing force or tension underneath the benefit
- Whether there's a **gatekeeper** (a buyer, editor, or client who has to choose to use and champion the design before it reaches anyone), and what would make them motivated to act
- Any early concept ideas you already have
- Whether the output should be mood boards or rough story comps

**What you get for each of the three concepts:**

- A one-line concept summary with a "glimmer of excitement" and a note on gatekeeper appeal
- Story elements: protagonist type, conflict, surprise flavor, and the named "aha/get-it moment"
- Visual toolbox choices: symbolism, color/mood, composition, style/era reference, and typography approach
- A storyboard-ready image prompt (one prompt for a mood board, or one per frame for story comps)

**Closes with** a one-paragraph comparison note explaining how the three concepts differ across protagonist type, creative strategy, surprise flavor, and emotional register, so the distinction is clear at a glance.

## Core Concepts Used Across Both Prompts

These frameworks share a common story foundation worth understanding:

| Concept | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Protagonist** | Whoever "the events of the story mean the most to" (often not the person pictured) |
| **Conflict** | The predator in the bushes; the threat or tension the protagonist faces |
| **Surprise** | The aha/get-it moment: the small intellectual gap the viewer closes themselves |
| **Symbolism** | Familiar shorthand objects; don't avoid the obvious, because familiarity is what makes the aha possible |
| **Gatekeeper** | The person who must choose to use or distribute the design before it reaches anyone |
| **Protagonist word** | The single word in your typography the reader must see first and feel most |
