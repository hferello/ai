**Ridgewell Partners** — a small consultancy helping a mid-size retailer fix a broken client onboarding process.

## Context file

```markdown
# Project Context

> **One-liner:** Cut new-client onboarding from six weeks to three without adding headcount — by fixing handoffs, not by working faster.

## Mission

Help Northgate Retail onboard new B2B clients with fewer errors and less back-and-forth. The win is clients live on schedule and the ops team stops firefighting the same issues every month.

## Principles

- Fix the process before adding tools. No new software until we know where the breakdowns are.
- Speak in outcomes the client cares about — time to live, error rate — not internal jargon.
- Every recommendation must name an owner and a date. Ideas without owners do not ship.
- Conservative with client data. Use anonymised examples in any external document.

## Must-Haves

1. A current-state map of the onboarding journey with pain points marked.
2. A prioritized list of fixes (quick wins vs structural changes).
3. A 90-day implementation plan the client's ops lead can run without us in the room.

## Constraints

- Eight-week engagement. Two consultants, roughly 12 hours per week combined.
- Client budget is fixed — no scope for a full systems integration project.
- Primary stakeholders: VP Operations (sponsor), Onboarding Manager (day-to-day), IT liaison (part-time).
- All deliverables need VP sign-off before they go to the wider team.

## Out of Scope

- Vendor selection or RFP for new software.
- Changes to pricing, contracts, or sales handoff.
- Training materials or LMS rollout (we can recommend, not build).
- Work outside the B2B onboarding track (consumer returns, warehouse ops, etc.).

## Standards

- Documents: executive summary first, detail in appendices.
- Numbers must cite a source — interview, report, or ticket data. No invented statistics.
- Use the client's terms: "account setup" not our internal label "client provisioning".
- Slide decks: one idea per slide, no walls of bullet points.

## Success Criteria

- Onboarding cycle time drops from six weeks to four within 90 days of plan kickoff.
- Error rate on first invoice drops by at least 30%.
- Onboarding Manager says the plan is actionable without weekly consultant support.
```

## Work brief: Onboarding improvement plan

```markdown
# Work Brief: 90-day onboarding improvement plan

## 1. Overview

A written plan for Northgate Retail's ops team to implement onboarding fixes over the next 90 days. This is the main deliverable of the engagement — the journey map and pain-point analysis feed into it, but this document is what the client will actually run.

## 2. Goals

- Give the Onboarding Manager a sequenced list of changes they can execute.
- Separate quick wins (under two weeks) from changes that need IT or policy approval.
- Secure VP Operations sign-off so the plan has authority behind it.

## 3. Audience

- Primary reader: Onboarding Manager — needs step-by-step clarity, not strategy fluff.
- Approver: VP Operations — needs the first two pages to stand alone.
- Secondary: IT liaison — needs to see what systems or access changes are implied.

## 4. Requirements

1. Executive summary (one page): problem, target state, top five actions.
2. Current-state summary with three to five root causes — not a full journey map (that is a separate doc).
3. Recommended changes grouped as: quick wins, process fixes, structural changes.
4. For each change: owner role, effort estimate, dependency, success measure.
5. 90-day timeline in phases (days 1–30, 31–60, 61–90).
6. Risks and mitigations — at least three, including "no bandwidth to implement."

## 5. Non-Goals (Out of Scope)

- A full financial ROI model (rough order-of-magnitude is enough).
- Detailed IT specs or API documentation.
- Change-management comms or training scripts.

## 6. Stakeholder Considerations

- VP cares about cycle time and error rate — lead with those metrics.
- Onboarding Manager is sceptical of past consultants — tone should be practical, not theoretical.
- IT liaison is overloaded — flag anything that needs their time early in the timeline.

## 7. Practical Considerations

- Draft due for internal Ridgewell review by March 10.
- Client presentation March 15 — plan must be VP-ready by then.
- Use anonymised ticket examples only; client name OK in headers, not in case studies.

## 8. Success Metrics

- VP approves the plan in the March 15 meeting or with one round of edits.
- At least four of five "quick wins" are marked as started within 30 days of kickoff.
- Plan fits in roughly 12–15 pages excluding appendices.

## 9. Open Questions

- Does IT require a formal change request for the CRM field updates?
- Is the Onboarding Manager the right owner for the data-cleanup workstream, or should it sit with RevOps?
```
