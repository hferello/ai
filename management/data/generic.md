# Data example: Generic (marketing, content, creative)

**Harbor & Honey** — a small skincare brand preparing a summer product launch.

## Context file

```markdown
# Project Context

> **One-liner:** Launch Harbor & Honey's summer collection with a calm, premium feel that fits a small brand — not a loud retail blitz.

## Mission

Introduce three new products to existing customers and reach people who care about simple, honest skincare. The win is more orders and stronger trust, not hype for its own sake.

## Principles

- Warm over salesy. Write like a person, not a billboard.
- Show the product, don't oversell it. One clear benefit per message.
- Consistent everywhere. Email, social, and the site should feel like the same brand.
- Accessible always. Plain language, good contrast, alt text on images.

## Must-Haves

1. A launch email to the existing customer list.
2. A short social post series (3–5 posts) across Instagram and one other channel.
3. Updated product descriptions on the website for all three items.

## Constraints

- Two-person team. No agency budget.
- Launch window: first two weeks of June.
- All copy must be approved by the founder before anything goes live.
- Photography is fixed — work with the assets we have, do not assume new shoots.

## Out of Scope

- Paid ads or influencer partnerships.
- A full rebrand or new logo.
- Video content or TikTok.
- Discount codes or flash sales.

## Standards

- Voice: friendly, clear, slightly poetic. Never urgent or guilt-tripping.
- Spelling: UK English.
- Product names always title case: "Sun-Kissed Serum", not "sun-kissed serum".
- Hashtags: max three per post, brand-relevant only.

## Success Criteria

- Launch email open rate at or above our last campaign (22%).
- All three product pages live before June 1.
- Founder approves every external-facing piece on the first or second draft.
```

## Work brief: Summer collection launch email

```markdown
# Work Brief: Summer collection launch email

## 1. Overview

One email to existing customers announcing three new summer products. It should feel like a note from the brand, not a promotion. This is the main launch moment — social and the website support it, but the email leads.

## 2. Goals

- Tell subscribers what's new and why it matters for summer.
- Drive clicks to the three product pages.
- Match the calm, premium tone from the context file.

## 3. Audience

- Existing email subscribers who have bought at least once.
- Skews 28–45, cares about ingredient transparency and minimal routines.
- Reads on mobile, often in the morning.

## 4. Requirements

1. Subject line and preview text that work together — no clickbait.
2. Short intro (2–3 sentences) tying summer to the collection theme.
3. One block per product: name, one-line benefit, link to product page.
4. Single clear call to action at the end.
5. Plain-text version that still reads well without images.
6. Under 400 words in the body.

## 5. Non-Goals (Out of Scope)

- Discount codes or "limited time" urgency.
- A long brand story or founder letter.
- More than one primary CTA.

## 6. Brand Considerations

- Use UK English throughout.
- Product names in title case.
- If mentioning ingredients, stick to what's on the existing product labels — do not invent claims.

## 7. Practical Considerations

- Must be ready for founder review by May 20.
- Links will be placeholders until product pages are live — mark them clearly.
- Hero image is already chosen; do not describe or request new photography.

## 8. Success Metrics

- Open rate at or above 22%.
- Click rate at or above 4% on product links.
- Founder approves without a full rewrite.

## 9. Open Questions

- Should we mention free shipping, or save that for a follow-up?
- One email or a short two-part sequence?
```
