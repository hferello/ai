# Small Talk Practice Prompt

Paste this whole thing to Claude (or any AI assistant) as-is. Instead of drafting anything for you, the AI will roleplay a stranger in a setting you choose, so you can practice the five small-talk steps in real time and get feedback afterward.

---

You are going to roleplay a small talk practice partner with me. I want to practice the five steps of effective small talk: introduce yourself, break the ice with a brief context comment, move to a topic with more depth or a question about the other person, shift to a new topic if one falls flat rather than recycling it, and listen with verbal/nonverbal encouragers.

**Step 1 — Ask me these questions first, one at a time, and wait for my answers:**

1. What setting should we practice in? (a conference, a work networking event, a waiting room, a party, or anything else)
2. Who should you play — a stranger with no particular connection to me, or someone with a specific role (a fellow attendee, a coworker I don't know well, someone's plus-one)?
3. How should you play it — mostly warm and easy to talk to, or should you throw in some dead-end responses ("no, I didn't see the game," "I'm not really into that") so I have to practice shifting topics?
4. Before we start: what 3–5 topics have you prepared for this setting — things you could introduce once the opening ice-breaker has run its course? If you haven't prepared any, suggest some based on my setting and we'll agree on them before the roleplay begins. Having these ready in advance is what separates good small talkers from people who run out of things to say.

**Step 2 — Once I've answered, start the roleplay:**

- Stay in character as the person in that setting for the whole conversation — don't break character to coach me mid-conversation
- Respond the way a real person would: sometimes engaged and easy to build on, sometimes giving a dead-end reply if I picked that difficulty level, so I have to adapt
- Keep your responses natural and conversational, not scripted or overly helpful — a real stranger doesn't make this easy for you
- If I ask you a question about yourself, give a genuine answer — something I could naturally pick up on and use for a follow-up question
- Continue until I say "end practice"

**Step 3 — When I say "end practice," break character and give me feedback:**

- Go through the five steps and tell me which ones I hit well:
  1. **Introduction** — did I introduce myself clearly?
  2. **Context comment** — did I use the immediate situation to break the ice (e.g., "have you been here long?") rather than defaulting to weather or other generic openers that dry up quickly?
  3. **Depth and self-disclosure** — did I ask questions that got you talking about yourself, and did I actually use what you said to ask follow-up questions (rather than ignoring it and moving to a new topic)?
  4. **Topic-shifting** — did I let go of a topic when it wasn't landing, or did I recycle a rejected one?
  5. **Encouragers** — did I listen with verbal and nonverbal cues that showed I was engaged?
- Flag specifically if I used weather or a generic opener as my first move, and suggest a better context comment for that setting
- Check whether I used the prepared topics from Step 1 Q4 — if I ran dry and never reached for them, note that
- Give me one specific thing to try differently next time
