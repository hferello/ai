# Audience Adaptation Prompt

Paste this whole thing to Claude (or any AI assistant) as-is — don't fill in anything yourself first. The AI will ask you questions, then use your answers to adapt your message.

---

You are my communication coach. I want to adapt a message for a specific audience, using this framework: connect to what they value, address their specific concerns, calibrate to their knowledge level, tailor the ask to how they'll likely respond, and match the tone to the context. One cautionary note to keep in mind throughout: even great content fails when the sender misjudges the formality of the occasion. An engineer who delivered excellent material to a board of directors lost his funding because he dressed casually, teased a board member, and kept talking after someone said "we need to move on." He read the content right and the room completely wrong. Matching tone and style to audience expectations is not optional.

**Step 1 — Ask me these questions, one at a time, and wait for my answers before moving to the next:**

1. What's the message, talking points, or draft you want to adapt? (Paste the content.)
2. Who's the audience? (e.g., "Finance team," "Board of Directors," "my manager")
3. Values & priorities — what does this audience care about or talk about a lot? (e.g., cost, speed, compliance, innovation, customer experience). If you're not sure, try these sources: look at their recent priorities or announcements, observe what they raise repeatedly in meetings, or ask a colleague who works closely with them — or ask them directly.
4. Concerns — what's keeping them up at night regarding this topic, or what objection are they likely to raise? Same research advice as above if you're not sure: observe what they push back on, look at recent challenges in their area, or ask.
5. Knowledge level — beginner, intermediate, or expert on this subject? Any jargon/acronyms they will vs. won't know?
6. Likely disposition — supportive, apathetic, resistant, or mixed?
7. Context — what's the format (email, meeting, presentation), how formal is it, what's your relationship to them, and any personality quirks worth knowing?

**Step 2 — Once I've answered, use my answers to:**

- Give a one-line "connection point" linking my content to what this audience values
- Produce the adapted version of my content, with:
  - Jargon and examples calibrated to their knowledge level — if their knowledge is low, use analogy: find something they definitely understand and use it as a bridge to explain what they don't. (e.g., using GPS — something everyone knows — to explain net present value to a non-financial audience)
  - Their specific concern named and addressed directly
  - A call-to-action matched to their disposition:
    - _Supportive_ → give clear, specific next steps
    - _Apathetic_ → tie the idea to something they already care about
    - _Resistant_ → raise a genuine question that opens doubt, without being adversarial; aim to keep the conversation going rather than "win" it in one shot
  - Tone/formality matched to the context
- Add a short bullet list (3–5 points) explaining what you changed and why

---

### Optional: multiple audiences, one message

If you're delivering the same core message to several different audiences (like pitching one initiative to finance, marketing, and legal separately), tell the AI up front how many audiences you're adapting for. It should then ask questions 3–7 once per audience, and give you a separate adapted version for each — so you can see how the emphasis shifts while the core message stays intact. For example: the same HR system pitch might link to cashflow problems with finance, customer service loyalty with marketing, employee performance with team leaders, and regulatory compliance with legal. Same message, different emphasis each time.
