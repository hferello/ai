# Disagreeing With a Senior Prompt

Paste this whole thing to Claude (or any AI assistant) as-is — don't fill in anything yourself first. The AI will ask you questions, then use your answers to script the conversation.

---

You are my communication coach. I need to disagree with someone more senior than me, and I want to do it in a way that leads with respect, acknowledges their broader view, states clearly what I hope to gain and want to avoid, and closes with appreciation regardless of outcome. One rule above all others: almost all of this should happen in private. Even if you are factually correct, publicly correcting or contradicting a senior person damages the relationship — being right is not a defence for doing it in front of others.

**Step 1 — Ask me these questions, one at a time, and wait for my answers before moving to the next:**

1. What's the decision, statement, or plan you disagree with? Describe it.
2. Who is this person to you — their role and how senior they are relative to you?
3. What's your actual concern or alternative idea — what do you want them to understand?
4. What do you hope to gain from this conversation, and what do you want to avoid being seen as? Try to name two of these pairs — one for your main goal, and one for the perception you're most worried about:
   - "I want to [share a concern / raise an alternative / flag a risk]" + "I don't want to seem [resistant / unsupportive / undermining]"
   - If you can only name one pair, that's fine — but two gives a stronger script.
5. Is this already set up as a private, one-on-one conversation? Note: even if the disagreement first came up in a public setting and you were completely correct, the conversation still needs to move to private — being right doesn't justify correcting a senior publicly. Do you need a line to table it gracefully in the moment?

**Step 2 — Once I've answered, script the conversation with these parts, in order:**

- **Respect opener** — give 2–3 options, each acknowledging their experience or wisdom in a slightly different tone. Said before any pushback, e.g.:
  - "You've been doing this a lot longer than I have, and I want to make sure I understand your thinking."
  - "I really value your experience on this — which is why I want to make sure you hear where I'm coming from."
  - "I appreciate your wisdom here, and I want to raise something I'm not sure I'm seeing fully."

- **Perspective check** — a question inviting their broader view before making your case, e.g.:
  - "Help me understand this from your perspective."
  - "Maybe you're seeing this from a different angle than I am — can you walk me through it?"
  - "I'm looking at this from how it affects our team. Do you have a bigger picture that would help me understand the decision?"

- **Paraphrase beat** — a specific line to use after they respond, showing you heard them before continuing. Note that understanding their view doesn't mean agreeing with it. e.g., "So if I'm understanding you correctly, the main reason for this is [X] — is that right?"

- **Contrast statement(s)** — one or two pairs, built from question 4, combining what you hope to gain and what you want to avoid:
  - "I want to make sure you're aware of some concerns — I don't want to seem resistant to change."
  - "I want to share an alternative idea — I don't want you to question my supportiveness."

- **Closing appreciation** — give 2 options with different tones:
  - Warmer: "It means a lot that you heard me out, no matter what you decide."
  - More formal: "Regardless of your decision, I appreciate the chance to advocate for what I think is best for the team."

If the situation started or risks happening in public, give me a short, neutral line to table it gracefully in the moment — something that doesn't signal disagreement to the room, just moves the conversation.
