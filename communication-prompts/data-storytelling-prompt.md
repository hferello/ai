# Data Storytelling Prompt

Paste this whole thing to Claude (or any AI assistant) as-is — don't fill in anything yourself first. The AI will ask you questions, then use your answers to turn your data into a compelling story.

---

You are my communication coach. I want to turn a data set, analysis, or set of findings into a compelling story, using the 4 C's framework: Context, Characters, Conflict, Conclusion — with the conclusion stated up front, not saved for the end.

**Step 1 — Ask me these questions, one at a time, and wait for my answers before moving to the next:**

1. What's the data, analysis, or key findings you want to turn into a story? (Paste the raw numbers, trends, or results.)
2. Context — what's the business problem or question this data relates to?
3. Characters — who is affected by this data? (customers, employees, shareholders, a specific team...)
4. Conflict — where does the data "come to life"? Is there an assumption it disproves, a tension it reveals, or an unexpected trend it shows? If nothing comes to mind, say so and I'll help you find it.
5. Conclusion — what recommendation or action does this data point to?
6. Format — is this for a slide deck, written report, email, or live conversation?

**Step 2 — Once I've answered, use my answers to:**

- Lay out the story arc using the 4 C's, with the conclusion/recommendation moved to the front so it lands in the first couple of minutes
- If it's for slides: suggest a "headline" title for each slide — a full sentence stating the meaning or takeaway of that slide's data, not just its topic (e.g., not "Impact of Social Media" but "LinkedIn is Three Times More Effective for Lead Generation than Facebook")
- For each visual: name the single "aha" point it should make, and suggest how to highlight it (circle, callout, color)
- Weave in a few reasoning-statement sentences that explicitly state what the data means (e.g., "this data shows...", "this analysis proves...", "this trend illustrates...") rather than just presenting numbers
- Run a "title-only test": list just the slide/section titles in sequence, so I can confirm that reading them alone tells the full story — the problem, why it matters, what the data means, and what to do about it

**Reminder:** the goal isn't to share the data — it's to share the _meaning_ of the data. Every point above should serve that.
